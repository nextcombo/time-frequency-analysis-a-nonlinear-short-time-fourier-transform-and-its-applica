# Time-Frequency Analysis: A Nonlinear Short-Time Fourier Transform and Its Application in Instantaneous Frequency Estimation [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction In the field of fault diagnosis for rotating machinery, signal processing techniques are widely used to identify features closely related to mechanical failures [1] and [2]. Faults in recorded vibration and sound signals typically manifest as transient characteristics that appear within a short period of time. Considering that different fault signals occupy different frequency bands, Time-Frequency Analysis (TFA) is an effective tool for characterizing non-stationary TF features of transient faults [4]. Although direct applications of TFA methods have been reported in many studies, the inherent shortcomings of classical TFA methods have never been effectively addressed. Linear TFA methods, such as Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) and Wavelet Transform (WT), calculate the inner product between signals and basis functions with TF feature localization capabilities. However, due to the lack of TF basis functions that can be compressed and supported, linear TFA methods exhibit poor capabilities to represent precise TF features. Dual-linear TFA methods, such as Wigner-Ville distribution and Cohen class distribution, are used to compute localized signal-related Fourier transforms. However, the cross terms in dual-linear TFA methods significantly limit their application. The deficiencies in traditional fault tree analysis methods reduce the diagnostic system's sensitivity to early weak faults and strong noise faults. To enhance the capability of TFA methods in detecting faults in complex environments, advanced methods have been proposed and introduced in the past decade, such as Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) [5], Spectral Peak Ratio (SK) method [6] and [7], and Synchronous Compression Transform (SST) [8]-[10].
The SK method is a technique based on the peak kurtosis index to extract the most transient components. Peak kurtosis is a statistical measure used to quantify the temporal discreteness of time series signals, and it can also be used to detect transients contained in faulty signals. The SK method first requires extending a one-dimensional signal to a two-dimensional TF plane based on STFT or bandpass filters, which allows for the reconstruction or selection of components that are most correlated with the fault with the highest peak kurtosis. Benefiting from the sensitivity of the peak kurtosis index to transient faults, the SK method has shown effectiveness in diagnosing mechanical faults [15], [16].
The SST method has been introduced as a post-processing tool for linear TFA methods and has been applied to fault diagnosis in rotating machinery [10]. The purpose of the SST is to obtain a clearer TF representation that can characterize faults at high TF resolution. Simultaneously, transient components can be extracted from more sharpened TF results. To extract signals from the SST results, it is necessary first to estimate the corresponding IF trajectory corresponding to the transient component. However, this is challenging due to the fact that fault signals often do not meet the requirements of the weak time-varying SST framework. Moreover, undesired background noise can cause severe interference in the SST results, potentially preventing accurate characterization of the IF. To further improve the performance of the SST, advanced methods have been proposed, such as demodulation SST [17], matching SST [18], higher-order SST [19], and synchronous extraction transform (SET) [4].
From the introduction above, we can see that many advanced techniques have been introduced to extract transient components from raw signals, which is an important issue to enhance the fault detection capability of diagnostic systems. In this paper, we propose a new TFA method that can precisely describe transient features on the TF plane and extract them in the time domain. This method is compared with advanced fault diagnosis methods and their improved versions, including SK, EMD, SST, etc. The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides a detailed theoretical explanation of our proposed method. In Section 3, Rényi entropy and kurtosis metrics are used to illustrate the quantitative comparison of TF results produced by different TFA methods. Experimental validation is presented in Sections IV and V. The conclusion is presented in Section VI.
